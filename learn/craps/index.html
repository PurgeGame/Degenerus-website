Craps Autobattle

Build one ten-chip craps board, send it into a scheduled battle, and watch every entrant run against the same dice. You are choosing a strategy for an automated bankroll race, not calling bets roll by roll.

The two-minute version

  1. Choose anywhere from zero through seven chips. You can put up to three chosen chips on one betting spot.
  2. The protocol scatters random chips until every entry has a ten-chip board. Leaving more to chance also raises the odds of a Hot Shooter profit boost.
  3. Pick a scheduled battle and burn the required FLIP to enter. Your finished board repeats for each shooter.
  4. The run starts with enough bankroll for five base boards and targets 5× that starting bankroll.
  5. Reach the target to earn Goal status. Miss it and eventually stop as a Bust. Goal always ranks above Bust, but your run keeps going after Goal to establish its high point.

Build the board

The board supports Pass Line, Don’t Pass, Place 4/5/6/8/9/10, Hard 4, and Hard 8. You cannot submit both Pass and Don’t Pass on the same chosen board, although random scatter can create combinations you would not pick yourself. Fewer chosen chips mean more variance and a better Hot Shooter chance; more chosen chips mean more control over the strategy. Neither route guarantees a stronger run.

The odds below are profit odds. A 2:1 win adds two units of profit for each unit on the spot; the original stake remains part of the bankroll.

How the automated run works

Everybody in a battle receives the same shared dice sequence. That keeps the comparison clean, but it also means player outcomes are correlated: a rough sequence can punish much of the field at once. Each resolved board is replayed across successive shooters, and the mandatory board cost doubles every three shooters. The longer the run lasts, the more bankroll it must put at risk.

If your bankroll cannot fund the next board but can cover at least half, the engine gives it a fair survival flip. Win and the bankroll doubles so play can continue; lose and the run Busts at zero. Falling below half the required board cost is an immediate Bust with the remainder recorded for ranking.

At a shooter boundary, reaching 5× the starting bankroll permanently latches Goal status and protects the goal reserve. The simulation keeps running to find the run’s high point. That distinction matters: reaching Goal makes a run payable, while pushing higher is how it tries to win the field.

Ranking and settlement

Every Goal beats every Bust. Goal runs rank by high point. Bust runs rank by shooters survived, then by whether anything was kept, then by high point and raw ending bankroll, and finally by a deterministic tiebreaker. Even when the entire field Busts, one entry still ranks first and wins the battle bounty.

A Goal run’s individual return is paid as Coinflip stake credit, not immediately liquid FLIP. That credit still faces the communal daily Coinflip before it can become claimable. A Bust pays zero even when its recorded ending bankroll is above zero. Separately, the top-ranked entry wins the field’s battle pot, built from entrant bounties and any admitted boost or donation. A qualifying winner can also take a shot at the Run It Up progressive.

High Roller

High Roller uses the day’s 10× or 100× multiplier: it burns that multiple of the entry and scales the individual Goal return. It does not buy a luckier dice stream or a better board simulation. The same unscaled run establishes rank, while High Roller also competes on its sideboard. It magnifies exposure as directly as it magnifies a payable result.

What you can lose

The entry is burned up front. A Bust returns nothing, a Goal can still miss the battle bounty, and its credited return takes another round of Coinflip risk.

Shared dice do not make the game safe; they make the tournament comparable. Random scatter, Hot Shooter, survival flips, escalating board costs, and the daily High Roller multiplier all add variance. Treat every entry as FLIP you can lose in full.
