WWXRP

Wacky Wrapped XRP. The contract's own documentation reads, in capital letters, "THIS IS A JOKE TOKEN!" It has no protocol backing asset or redemption path; its utility is inside the game.

How you end up with it

Source What you get
Losing the daily coinflip 1 WWXRP consolation for the burned FLIP stake
Lootbox rolls A Degenerette spin staking 1 WWXRP on 15% of outcome rolls
Presale boxes A 1-in-10 roll pays a flat 1 WWXRP, whatever the box cost
Foil pack faces 20% of winning claims use the face count as the stake for a WWXRP Degenerette spin

The punchline pays

Degenerette has a dedicated WWXRP lane, and it is openly rigged in the player's favor: the reel forces extra matches, and its token-denominated expected payout curve runs from 70% at score zero to 120% at the cap. Because WWXRP has no protocol backing or redemption path, those percentages are not monetary returns.

And one prize is real: be the first to hit the maximum score of 9 on a WWXRP spin in a 10-level bracket and you win half a whale pass. One per bracket, so the first 9 takes it. The joke token can pay out a piece of the most serious position in the game.

The daily burn draw

Burn at least 25 WWXRP to enter that day's draw. Your weight is the whole WWXRP you burned, scaled by your activity score, and you can enter as many times a day as you like. Each day rolls two gates: a 1-in-365 BIG prize, and failing that a 1-in-30 SMALL prize. When a gate hits and the winning bucket holds entries, one weighted entrant takes it. BIG pays 100,000 FLIP, SMALL pays 10,000 FLIP, both credited as a coinflip stake rather than a wallet payout. An empty winning bucket is a dud day, so the joke token still has to show up to win.

The century incinerator

Every hundredth level triggers a special event. WWXRP burned on the run-up to a century can turn into real ETH, but only if that century's Big-Ass Flip comes up empty. The rest you find out at level 100.

Why it exists

WWXRP is internal game material for consolation awards and high-variance spins, without a contract promise of backing or redemption. It can still have opportunity cost or a speculative market price; the protocol itself promises neither. The name is a bonus.
