Why This Game Doesn't Die

A summary of the game-theoretic analysis of Degenerus Protocol

Degenerus is an on-chain lottery on Ethereum where no operator takes a cut of wagers. Player deposits are pooled and redistributed entirely as prizes. The pooled ETH earns staking yield from Lido, split in equal quarters between a player jackpot accumulator, sDGNRS holders, the creator's vault, and community-voted charities. This external yield makes the game slightly positive-sum for players as a whole. The rules are immutable smart contract code: no one can pause the game, drain the funds, or change the rules after deployment. Most players will lose money, but skilled play can show a positive expectation, every player has a fair chance to win big, and nobody can be rugged.

This page summarizes a 60-page paper that stress-tests the theory that the protocol is nearly impossible to kill. Every claim below is argued in the full paper and verifiable against the open-source contract code.

How the game works

The game progresses through levels. Each level has an ETH target that its prize pool must reach. Players buy tickets (entries into the current level's jackpot) or luckbox (randomized packages of future-level tickets, tokens and boons, with value that scales with consistent activity). Ticket purchases send most of their ETH to the current level's pool. Luckbox purchases send most to a reserve called the futurepool. Players also earn FLIP, a token that can be burned to buy tickets at any level. Because 1,000 FLIP always buys one ticket regardless of price, FLIP grows in value as levels advance and ticket prices rise. (Appendix C, §6.1)

While a level is filling, daily jackpots pay out to ticket holders. When the pool hits its target, larger jackpot draws run over three days (one day when the level fills on its first day or is an x5 Decimator level), culminating in a single big winner on the final day. The game then advances to the next level with a higher target. If a level fails to fill within 30 days (250 at level 1), GAMEOVER triggers: after any deity pass refunds, everything the contract holds that is not already owed is paid to that level's ticket holders. Pooled ETH leaves only as prizes, on the game's schedule, according to the game's rules. (§1)

Selfish players build the collective

Degens are willing to lose money for jackpot thrills. Their deposits fill prize pools and fund the jackpots that keep the game exciting. Grinders are willing to lock capital and embrace variance for a return on their investment. Their luckbox purchases inflate the futurepool, which funds future jackpots and mechanical defenses. Affiliates recruit new players for FLIP commissions, delivering the growth the system requires. Each type gets what they want. What they give up in exchange is exactly what the others need. Staking yield on locked capital means total payouts exceed total deposits. (§2.1–§2.3)

If too many grinders pile in, the surplus shrinks and returns fall. Because grinders are price-sensitive by definition, the worst performers leave, restoring returns for whoever remains. Entertainment seekers are indifferent to grinder population. The system self-corrects. Meanwhile, every profitable strategy requires variance tolerance and sustained engagement, which structurally excludes the passive capital that compresses returns in simpler systems. (§2.3, §2.4)

Exits make the returns stronger

When a player stops participating, they still collect prizes they are owed, but only on the game's schedule. Their deposited ETH stays in the pools until it is paid out as prizes. Their absence means less competition for jackpot draws and daily payouts. In the standard crypto death spiral, exits drain the treasury. Here, exits reduce the number of players splitting it. (§8.1, §8.2)

The game runs itself

From level 2 on, the futurepool drip fires daily on one permissionless call that the contract pays a bounty for, moving ETH from the reserve into the current level's pool. If the futurepool holds about 1.9 times the level's opening shortfall, the drip alone fills the pool before the 30-day deadline. On testnet run 68, after all external buying stopped, the drip completed three levels in a row, and GAMEOVER came 93 days after the last purchase. (§9.2)

Because luckbox direct most of each purchase to the futurepool, grinder activity massively inflates this reserve. The individually optimal strategy (buy luckbox early, maintain a high degen score through regular purchases) is exactly the strategy that funds the mechanical defense. A population of optimizers produces maximum resilience as a side effect of self-interested play. (§12.2)

The closer it gets to death, the harder it is to kill

If a level stalls and its GAMEOVER deadline approaches, buying tickets for the stalling level becomes increasingly profitable. If GAMEOVER fires, nearly all ETH remaining in the protocol is distributed to those ticket holders. The terminal payout per ticket far exceeds the ticket cost: on run 68's failing level it paid 3.7 times face in ETH. (§10.1)

An early buyer during a stall has positive expected returns even if GAMEOVER never happens. Daily payouts from the drip and the level's regular jackpot alone cover the ticket cost. The terminal payout is pure upside. Even a final-day buyer needs little conviction: on run 68's failing level the trade broke even at about an 8% chance of GAMEOVER. (§10.1)

The key design choice: the only way to claim a share of the terminal payout is to hold tickets for the stalling level, and FLIP cannot buy them while the level is filling. Every ticket bought sends ETH directly into the pool that needs filling to prevent GAMEOVER. Positioning for the death payout and preventing death are the same transaction. The more likely death appears, the more people buy in, and the less likely death becomes. (§10.2, Appendix E)

The Resilience Thesis. Once a non-trivial amount of locked ETH exists, the incentive structure makes continued level advancement the default outcome. The rational response to declining activity from other players is to increase one's own. As the probability of death rises, it becomes more individually profitable to take the actions that prevent it.

What this paper does not claim

It does not claim the game will succeed. The resilience thesis is conditional on accumulated locked capital. No mechanism design can create demand from nothing. Whether enough players show up initially is an empirical question the mechanism cannot answer. (§12.3)
