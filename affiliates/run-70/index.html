Degenerus Protocol · testnet run #70 · Base Sepolia · drawn to scale from on-chain data

Three levels finished with nobody buying. The fourth ran out of clock.

Buying stopped 96 blocks into Level 43 and never resumed. The last 535.2 ETH of purchases cleared Level 43. Levels 44, 45, and 46 then reached their targets with nobody buying, after 8, 13, and 28 game days, each slower than the last. From day 48 through day 134 the game credited ticketholders 6,113.5 ETH in jackpot awards. Then the reserve ran thin. Level 47 opened on day 104, 2,411.5 ETH short of a 3,257.5 ETH target, climbed for 30 days, and stopped 746.2 ETH short. On day 135 the game latched GAMEOVER and paid its entire remaining 4,364.4 ETH to 83 wallets in a single transaction. This is all 134 days of it.

Observed · levels 1 → 47 · days 2 → 134

Forty-three levels bought, three levels without a buyer, one level short

Each bar is a level: the pool it banked, the reserve behind it on the day it opened, and the insurance on top. The banked pool is levelPrizePool[L], written once when the level completes and never touched again, so the ladder only ever goes up: 154.1, 198.4, 226.5, then on to 3,149.1, 3,164.7, 3,173.4, and 3,257.5. The gold tick is the ratchet that level had to clear, which is simply the previous level's bar. The number under each bar is the days from that level opening to the next one. Levels came every 1 or 2 days while people were buying. Level 43 took 4, because its jackpot ran after the last purchase. Then they came every 10, 13, and 30 days with nobody buying, and the last one never came.

The reserve is the whole story: it peaks at 7,334.0 ETH on day 51, and falls with every level after that.The futurepool stood at 7,334.0 when Level 44 opened, 5,526.6 for Level 45, 3,828.3 for Level 46, and 3,137.9 for Level 47. By day 134 it held 947.6.

Observed · days 2 → 134 · every ETH the contract holds

The pools shrink after the last buyers, the total does not

The ladder counts pools only. This is the same run counting everything the contract holds: the claimable winnings credited to players and not yet claimed, the nextpool, the futurepool, and the insurance. The stack always adds to the contract's balance. After the last purchases the total barely moves: ETH leaves the pools as awards are credited and stays in the contract as claimable winnings. The total rose 546.9 from day 47 to day 51, 535.2 of it the last purchases, and 122.4 from day 51 to day 134. The contract's ETH balance did not rise in that second stretch. The growth is its stETH balance.

Total ETH held never fell after the last purchases: 20,547.8 on day 47, 21,094.7 on day 51, 21,217.1 on day 134.What fell is everything outside claimable winnings, from 9,708.9 on day 51 to 4,364.4 on day 134, while claimable winnings rose from 11,385.8 to 16,852.8. GAMEOVER pays out the first number and leaves the second claimable.

After the last buyers: three levels clear, the fourth fails, and the game pays out what is left

The whole stretch in one frame. The first bar is Level 43 as it opens, and the last purchases of the run arrive in the next 96 blocks, so the second bar already holds them and is taller than the first. The solid part of each bar is one day's pools: the nextpool building the next level, the reserve behind it, and the insurance on top. The dashed box above a stack is the net ETH that left the pools since the previous bar, so a bar's solid part plus its box reaches the top of the previous bar's solid part. It is net of everything that flowed back in, so it can be smaller than the awards credited, which the stall figures below give gross. Bar 7's box includes Level 45's Decimator, 457.1 ETH. Bars 3, 5, 7, and 9 are drawn once because each is a single day doing two jobs: one level's jackpot has finished paying and the next level's window begins. The figure under each total compares that bar with the same point one level earlier, named by its day. Each level's ratchet is drawn only across the window it governs, because it steps up every time. The tenth bar is hollow: Level 47 climbed for 30 days, reached 2,511.3, and stopped 746.2 short of a 3,257.5 ratchet. The last bar is the terminal drain: GAMEOVER empties every pool and credits 4,364.4 ETH across 305 awards.

Observed · game over · day 135 · block 47,984,795

It pays out everything it has left, in one transaction

GAMEOVER pays the pot out. The available balance, everything outside existing claims, is drained into a terminal jackpot for the cohort holding tickets at Level 47, the level that never opened.

83 wallets were paid, and one of them took 2,661.09 ETH of the 4,364.4.The largest single award was 2,618.63 ETH. The nextpool, futurepool, and current pool all read 0 afterwards, and 16,852.8 ETH of already credited winnings stayed claimable on top.

Provenance. Every figure is measured state, with no projections and no models. Pool bars come from the daily prize-pool snapshots of the Run 70 database. The drain is the terminal block's ETH jackpot awards, block 47,984,795. Values are display ETH, raw testnet wei ÷ 10¹², the run dashboard's normalization to mainnet ticket prices. A game day lasted 20 minutes in this run. Pool figures are the contract's own PrizePoolDailySnapshot events, the settled pools when each game day is sealed. Checked against the contract's pool views at the same blocks on 23 days, 70 of 92 values match to the wei. The rest differ by transfers made later in the same block, the largest being the 456.75 ETH described under Level 45. The insurance bar is the game's available balance outside the current, next, and future pools, which holds retained insurance and yield. The last day of Level 47's purchase phase is day 134, and GAMEOVER processed on day 135.

The ladder is read from chain, not inferred. Each level's banked pool is levelPrizePool[L], read as an eth_call to prizePoolTargetView() at the block that completed the level. That call returns the target of level L+1, which is exactly the pool level L banked, and the contract writes it once, at the level's transition. The Level 46 read, 3,257.5, is the target Level 47 had to reach, and it matches the target view recorded at the final level's opening and closing blocks.

What counts as a payout. Awards are the ETH jackpot awards credited between two daily snapshots, summed from recorded events. Ticket and pass awards are excluded, and so are Decimator claims, which are read separately from the Decimator claims table. The total is gross: winnings can be recycled into further play, and a credited prize need not have been withdrawn. It does not measure player profit. The terminal award sum leaves 46 original wei of rounding dust.

ETH in. Purchases are read from the chain, not from the indexed ETH-in table, which ends at the database cursor taken when the run was isolated, before Level 43 opened. 81 ETH-bearing transactions sent 535.2 ETH to the game between blocks 47,931,986 and 47,932,080, the last purchases of the run. Another 6 transactions in that window reverted, so their ETH never arrived and they are not counted. From block 47,932,719 to the terminal block no 100-block window shows a rise in the game's ETH balance, which is 11,385.8 from day 51 to the end. The contract's total balance is its ETH plus its stETH, checked at days 47, 51, 134. After day 51 the only part that grows is the stETH, from 9,708.9 to 9,831.4.

Numbering and timing. Every chart numbers a level by the tickets being bought, so Level 47's stall is the purchase phase at contract level 46. A level's transition lands at the day boundary, so the pools shown at target are the last snapshot before it: day 48 for Level 43, whose transition came on day 49. The five stall figures share one ETH-to-pixel scale.
