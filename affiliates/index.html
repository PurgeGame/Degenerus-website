The game, in sixty seconds

The core of the game is a progressive jackpot lottery: daily drawings pay ETH and other protocol assets to ticket holders. Ticket prices start at 0.01 ETH, and 100% of that money is returned to players. Once enough tickets have been sold, the main jackpot runs, paying far larger prizes for the next three days. Then the game level advances and the prior level's tickets expire worthless. Each new level requires larger inflows to complete, and at milestone levels the ticket price steps up. This is a simple lottery game on the surface, and can be played as such. Dig deeper and you'll find an economy engineered for immortality, where affiliates and investors are highly rewarded for supplying the attention and risk-seeking capital the system requires.

Only one market condition can cause GAMEOVER: if players fail to inflate the prize pool by the required amount in thirty days (250 for the first level), the game ends. To prevent this, the protocol banks a reserve using player capital and deploys it in two layers. The first is a daily drip that enriches ticketholders even if buying stalls, and can advance levels a limited number of times with no buyers at all. If that incentive is insufficient, the second layer takes over: a standing threat to pay ticketholders every ETH the game has left at GAMEOVER. This is designed so that the more certain the game is to die, the more profitable it is to buy a ticket.

The concept that allows this math to work: negative rake. No entity takes a cut of any bets. Every wei of wagered ETH is directed into the protocol's prize pools and that pooled ETH is converted to yield-bearing Lido stETH. The result is something no casino can offer: in this game, profitable strategies necessarily exist. 100% of entry fees plus 25% of the staking yield must eventually return to players as winnings. That surplus is guaranteed and someone has to capture it, but the potentially far larger profit center comes from "degen yield". The protocol is designed to extract from those who value action over profits, and use that money entirely to pay players above market value for providing the resources it requires.

Commissions are paid in FLIP, a transferable coin minted on top of each purchase, allowing all ETH to flow to the prize pools. The protocol values it at a fixed 1,000 per ticket, so ~20% of ETH volume means that for every five tickets your referrals purchase, you receive one ticket's worth of FLIP. Sell to gamblers and speculators, burn for ETH jackpot equity, or hold to realize a higher valuation as levels advance. Commissions, like all other FLIP rewards, must survive the daily ~double-or-nothing coinflip before becoming liquid. This averages out quickly, but makes one thing clear: in Degenerus Protocol, gambling is mandatory.

Most players with zero affiliate income will still lose money. This is a gambling game in which the luckiest players will be massive winners, and that money has to come from somewhere. Even optimal investment strategy, with a highly positive expected return, depends on rare jackpot wins to realize that profit. The only consistent, relatively low-variance income offered by the system comes from referral commissions, available freely to anyone who can bring in new money.

Your audience already wants this

It is also why the market has cooled: the sheep have been skinned enough times that their population is dwindling.

Degenerus Protocol is a direct response to this toxic meta. Its risks are synthetic and transparent. It is impossible for anyone to pause the game, alter the rules, or touch the money. Every winner is drawn using unmanipulable Chainlink VRF, so players may be lucky or unlucky, but they cannot be made victims. It is an indictment of the competition that "cannot rug you" qualifies as an innovation. Nevertheless, it is the one innovation that might bring the flock back to life, happy to be merely shorn.

The second demand is far older than crypto. Lottery players are willing to pay for a real chance of winning big, and they always will. The system is powered by their spending, and the affiliate program exists to bring them to this lottery: the one where nobody skims the pot, the jackpots only ever get bigger, and being a favorite is possible.

Degenerus requires only as much thought as the player wants to give it. Someone looking for a scratch-off or slot machine can buy action and chase a quick win. A relatively simple strategy of building degen score turns the game into a high-variance, high-return investment.

That strategy gets close to optimal play. Pursuing the remaining edges opens up a deep PvP game: maximizing expectation requires predicting what other players will do rather than following a fixed routine. The complexity is open-ended, but the game is turn-based: each turn lasts a day and players can make their moves in a minute.

Decisions affect expected returns, but luck dominates individual outcomes. Players have real agency, so winnings feel earned. Good players have an edge; bad players can still win big and believe they have one too.

Lottery players and crypto speculators feed each other. Both are buying the same thrill: life-changing upside, a decent chance of small wins, and high risk of loss. Lottery players supply the degen yield that makes investment worthwhile, while speculators chasing that yield lock up the capital that keeps jackpots growing. Affiliates supply the attention that brings both groups to the game. Bigger prizes attract more players, and the cycle is self-reinforcing.

The flow of money

Degenerus is a wealth transfer machine by design. Traditionally in crypto and other scams, money flows from bagholders to the top of the pyramid. That structure is brittle because it can only be fed one way: someone has to keep recruiting donators without them catching on that they're bagholders, and the moment the marketers start to run out of suckers, the system's death is inescapable. In contrast, here the "top" is a single person, whose main extraction is just a quarter of the stETH yield, and thus whose incentives are aligned entirely with long-term protocol health. The prize money itself strictly moves between players, in three transfers that each make the game harder to kill:

From the unlucky to the lucky. This transfer is the essence of gambling and why the game exists at all. Degens spend money for a chance to win big, generating the revenue that brings everyone else to the table. This variance masks the systemwide transfers: underdogs regularly win, and favorites can lose forever. There is a reason why high-stakes poker is a thing and high-stakes chess is not.

From casual gamblers to strategic investors. The game runs on locked capital. Deposits generate the external staking yield, and deep pools keep the jackpots regular. Placing yourself on the winning side of this transfer means locking increasing amounts of capital on uncertain timelines; it also means accepting the variance of getting paid primarily in lottery tickets. This heavy friction, combined with the fact that investors are competing with one another to divide up a fixed pot of degen yield, means that their returns are determined by an equilibrium rate that should reliably exceed that of more liquid assets.

From players without referral revenue to active affiliates. The game requires growth to live, and generating the necessary attention is skilled labor that demands real compensation. The program is designed to be attractive to professional marketers, but offers the same deal to everyone. Recruiting even a single active friend dramatically improves any player's outcomes. Affiliates who also wish to invest begin with an additional advantage: commission volume is a factor in the degen score formula that determines investment rewards, so players who engage with both systems will have the biggest edge.

Every gambling and crypto product ever built is designed to transfer wealth. Degenerus publishes the direction of the flows and lets you pick your seat. Degens are the customers: their spending purchases the thrill of gambling, and what they lose pays the two seats that make the game sustainable. Investors are paid over time for locking up the funds that keep the jackpots regular; once capital enters the contract, it stays there until paid out on the game's schedule as player winnings. Affiliates are paid well for the one job the code cannot do, bringing in the players. Even the most well-constructed perpetual wealth transfer machine is worth nothing if nobody knows it exists. Instead of bagholders, this protocol's donators are lottery degens who get what they paid for; instead of all the money flowing to scammers, the winners' seats are open to anyone willing to invest their labor or capital.

The difference between Degenerus Protocol and other crypto games is that it wasn't built as a wealth transfer thinly disguised as a game. The way the money flows is an emergent property of its primary objective: to be a game that deserves to grow forever because it offers its target audiences what they desire in any market conditions. Affiliates are rewarded generously because they are necessary for that growth, but the job is made easier by the fact that the product is engineered to push the buttons of degens as well as more strategic gamblers.

The whole economy on one page: ETH enters through ticket, luckbox, and pass purchases, cycles through the prize pools, and must eventually return to players as winnings. Purchases pay referral commissions in FLIP, which can be burned to enter the main jackpot drawings or the two special event jackpots, the Decimator and the Big-Ass Flip.

The only way it ends

Your referral code pays you for as long as your referrals keep playing, and nobody's referrals outlive the game. Nearly every other blockchain game dies quickly, so why is Degenerus different? The contract has one precisely defined market failure condition: the game ends only if a level's prize pool sits unfilled for thirty consecutive days, 250 for the first level. Assume that the game's death is imminent. Buying has collapsed to zero, the previous level completed entirely on the reserve drip, and every player agrees on what that means: this is the final level, the pool cannot fill, and the game will die in thirty days.

Anyone who believes the game is imminently doomed, current player or not, faces the same decision, and the rules of the game make the optimal choice obvious: buy as many tickets as you can afford on day one. GAMEOVER pays out all the game's remaining ETH to the final level's ticketholders, and until then the reserve drip pays them daily. The incentive to buy into that final jackpot is indirectly an incentive to save the game: every purchase helps fill the pool, the very outcome that restores the protocol's health.

Degenerus Protocol reverses the familiar crypto death spiral, in which decreased interest causes money to flee the ecosystem, further decreasing interest until there is no money left and nobody cares. That spiral needs an ending that is boring and foreseeable. Here the money cannot flee, and the doomsday clock keeps the foreseeable, a literal countdown, while deleting the boring: the only possible ending is a jackpot so large and profitable that its mere possibility generates its own interest and attracts new money.

Why it never gets that far

The test forced the worst case on purpose: 87 straight game days without a single new ETH purchase, while existing ticketholders kept collecting daily prizes and a terminal pot worth thousands of ETH sat in plain view. Buying in would have been massively profitable at any point, and the game needed only a small fraction of that pot to survive. Conditions half that lethal would kill any other crypto product on the spot.

In a normal market, with a healthy supply of profit-seeking individuals, the same mechanism runs continuously to ensure a steady inflow of ETH. The investor class is, by definition, the players who lock money into the game every day. Their primary edge comes from the length of a quest streak that demands exactly that cadence. Their total returns, as a class, are equal to the degen yield plus their share of the stETH yield, minus others' affiliate revenue. Within the class, individuals compete for that fixed per-level sum, and every one who quits raises the expected returns for all those who continue. Prior investments stay in the prize pools: exit means ceasing deposits or selling the whole account to sDGNRS at a deep discount. Importantly, these exits slow the game's pace, rather than draining its existing capital.

This player group is self-regulating, with a carrying capacity governed by the ratio of donating degens to the long-term investors farming them. Yet this capacity has a floor. Even losing every degen does not make quitting rational for the whole cohort: stored capital drains slowly through the worst downturn, what remains is worth fighting over, and a seat that pays off big if the gamblers come back has speculative value of its own.

Consider instead the reasonably optimistic scenario, because the same equilibrium that prevents the game's death doubles as a lever for its growth. Degenerus jackpots must grow: a fundamental rule of the game is that within each 100-level cycle, every level's prize pool has to exceed the one before it, and the only alternative is death. These cycles culminate in an especially massive jackpot that must double the previous century's. Jackpot size is the most reliable demand driver in gambling. Increased demand from degens means increased revenue for investors and affiliates, which attracts more of their capital and labor, which accelerates level advancement, prize growth, and degen attention. This loop is a life spiral and, unlike the death spiral, there are no circuit breakers designed to prevent it. Imagine what would happen if every Powerball jackpot were the largest in its history. Then imagine if you could earn a 20% commission on every ticket sold.

Why FLIP is worth something

Commissions pay in FLIP, so the fair question is whether FLIP is real money.

There are three ways to turn FLIP into ETH inside the game: burn it for tickets and win the main drawings, burn it into the Decimator's event jackpot, or flip it, since winning flips build the score that decides who wins Big-Ass Flip ETH when your tickets are drawn.

Outside the game, sell it: the bids come from players wanting those three conversions, degens gambling FLIP for its own sake in Degenerette, craps, or the daily flip, and speculators pricing the levels ahead, with the ticket peg setting the floor under all of it.

The anchor: 1,000 FLIP always buys one ticket. That never changes, while the ETH price of a ticket climbs a published curve, so if FLIP ever trades below the ticket rate, ticket buyers arbitrage the gap through the contract's end-of-level redemption window. Ticket demand holds the floor, and commission earned early can ride the whole curve.

1,000 FLIP buys a 0.01 ETH ticket at launch and a 0.24 ETH ticket at level 100. The FLIP rate never moves; the ticket price does.

That chart is approximately the floor, not the price. A token whose redemption value climbs should trade above it, most of all at launch: supply builds from zero, the whole 24x runway is ahead, and a 4x sits ten levels away. Every payout table in the game books FLIP at floor value, so whatever premium the market adds is paid on top. Every ETH ticket buy mints its buyer 100 FLIP, staked on the next daily flip, with bonuses for recycled winnings and bulk buys, and about a quarter of luckbox payouts arrive as FLIP. If FLIP is valued at double the floor, a purchase and coin dump combo claws back a large chunk of the buyin.

If this loop sounds familiar, it's the one the Ponzi minting games ran. The difference is that their coins minted more coin and had market value only because enough suckers unable to see the inevitable hyperinflationary conclusion were still willing to buy. This coin's supply is tied to the ETH players put into the game, and it has market value because it can be burned for ETH now, or later at a higher valuation. How much later is what prices the premium: the curve is denominated in levels, not days, so a faster game pulls every step closer and makes the climb worth more today. The people best positioned to speed the game up are the ones being paid in the coin.

FLIP has no faucet outside the game. Nearly all new FLIP is minted against ETH spending or as winnings on FLIP wagered with a negative expectation; the rest comes from small, bounded streams like daily quests, craps boosts, mining rewards and the seed stakes the vault and sDGNRS receive at launch and each century. It leaves through burns: ticket redemptions, Decimator entries, craps battles, Degenerette bets, and the dead half of every day's flips. Every FLIP is lucky, because all FLIP must win a coinflip to exist. Each day's supply of fresh FLIP, commissions included, either ~doubles or dies on the communal daily 50/50, so a single day's commission is a coin toss; over enough time the variance washes out to the quoted rate, delivered with a gambler's cadence.

All three conversion routes deepen with the game itself: more players means more ticket demand at the floor, and bigger pools mean bigger Decimator and BAF jackpots. The effective rate of FLIP:ETH conversion in these two drawings scales with a player's engagement with the game, so affiliates who are also active players gain an additional advantage. The protocol can afford 20% forever because commissions are minted in a coin whose supply growth depends on the activity that generates its value, so the bill scales with the results and can never outrun the game.
